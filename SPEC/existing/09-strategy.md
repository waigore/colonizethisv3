## Grand Scheme for Victory

Imperialism II can be won in a variety of different ways. When you begin a new game, it's best to decide on an overall grand scheme for victory soon after you start to play. This is especially important when playing on the Hard or Nigh-on Impossible settings. In difficult games it is important to waste no time or resources with non-productive actions.

Your grand scheme might be based on personal preference, on the terrain and resources available to you early in the game, and on events that occur in the first ten years or so.

---

## New World Approach: Friendly, Hostile, or Mixed

**1. Is your general intention toward the New World friendly or hostile or some of each?**

**Friendly approach:** If you plan to be friendly you need the Merchant Companies and Diplomatic Expertise technologies immediately. See [08. Technology](08-technology.md). You must get a Merchant unit to the New World as quickly as possible to protect provinces from invasion. This is especially important for tribal capitals. See [03. Civilian Units — Merchant](03-units-civilian.md#merchant) and [07. Diplomacy](07-diplomacy.md).

**Hostile approach:** If you plan to be hostile, you should build an extra Explorer and start looking for vulnerable wealthy provinces as soon as you can. The computer controlled Great Powers tend to invade and conquer provinces with gems and diamonds quite early. If you are planning on conquering in the New World, you do not want to fall behind early. See [03. Civilian Units — Explorer](03-units-civilian.md#explorer) and [04. Naval Units](04-units-naval.md).

Since the tribal capitals tend to be better defended, available gems and diamonds often remain in these provinces longer. So if you do fall behind in conquest, concentrate your prospecting and conquering efforts on the tribal capitals.

**Often a mixture of peace and war works best.** Balance development abroad with selective conquest where it yields the greatest benefit.

## Rapid Internal Development

**2. A second important point is the rapid internal development of your nation.**

Internal development includes:

- **Transport network** — Connect resources to your capital; build roads, ports, and eventually railroads. See [02. Economy — Transport](02-economy.md#transport-network).
- **Industry** — Produce materials from resources; balance production with trade needs. See [02. Economy — Industry](02-economy.md).
- **Labour force** — Recruit workers; train them when you have New World luxuries to sustain them. See [02. Economy — Labour Panel](02-economy.md#labour-panel).
- **Technology** — Invest in research; prioritise according to your grand scheme. See [08. Technology](08-technology.md).

**Money Lending** is an early technology that improves your financial flexibility. With the development of Money Lending, an empire can borrow more money and pay interest at a lower rate. This advance leads to superior bureaucratic organisation and, eventually, to upgrades in provincial artillery forces. See [08. Technology](08-technology.md) and [02. Economy — Credit and Debt](02-economy.md#credit-and-debt).

## Strategic Tensions

Throughout the game you face choices between:

- **New World investment vs Old World focus** — Since Old World provinces count toward victory, New World development should serve that end. See [00. Overview](00-overview.md).
- **Peaceful vs hostile expansion** — Alliances, embassies, and overseas profits vs conquest and plunder.
- **Cash vs commodities** — Selling materials for immediate profit vs using them for expansion (ships, regiments, infrastructure).
- **Defence vs offence** — Forts and garrisons consume resources; offensive attacks cost significant cash. See [06. Combat](06-combat.md) and [01. Overview](01-overview.md).
- **Military vs economic research** — Falling behind in military technology can be disastrous; economic and labour technologies enable faster expansion.
