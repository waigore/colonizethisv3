# A Young Monarch's Primer

## Purpose

You have inherited a Great Power in an age of discovery that runs from **1500 to about 1850**. Two maps matter: the **Old World**, where military victory is decided, and the **New World**, where colonies, tribes, and riches await. You issue decrees each turn; rival courts do the same. This chapter orients you to the premise, what winning means, the rhythm of a turn, and the game screen you will live in.

## How it is done

### The premise at a glance

- **Great Powers** (default six) are the only factions that submit orders and can win. You always play a Great Power.
- **Minor Nations** live in the Old World. They do not issue orders, but their provinces **count toward victory** for whoever owns them. They keep military parity with the strongest Great Power, so they are hard to swallow casually.
- **Tribes** live in the New World. Their provinces **do not** count toward the 31-province victory threshold. They are weaker militarily and can be contested without a formal declaration of war (unless another Great Power has invested in the province).
- Calendar pacing (default): turns 1–100 advance **two years** per turn (1500→1700); thereafter **one year** per turn. The HUD and turn summary show year as well as turn number.

### Victory at a glance

- **Military victory:** control **31 or more Old World provinces** (your ownership on those provinces). Checked at end of turn after all phases.
- **Calendar campaign end:** without military victory, the campaign can halt at calendar year **1800** (default mapping: turn **201**). That is not a military victory; observers may still name a declared winner by Great Power power score. **Infinite mode** (chosen at new game) bypasses the calendar halt; only military victory or quitting ends the campaign.

Later chapters deepen victory counsel; start here so you know the finish line.

### Rhythm of a turn

1. Issue orders and immediate actions on the map and empire screens while the phase is **Orders**.
2. Confirm **Next turn** when ready (see Chapter 14 for confirmation and news).
3. The engine resolves movement, combat, economy, diplomacy, market, build/work, and end-of-turn checks. Results appear when resolution finishes — not mid-click.
4. A new Orders phase begins (or victory / calendar halt stops further turns).

### Orientation tour of the game screen

1. From the shell, after a game is created or loaded, you arrive on **Game screen** — host for the map, chrome, next-turn flow, and overlays.
2. The map area is **Empire overview / map area**: pan, zoom, region tabs (Old World / New World), and selection of provinces and sea zones. When resource icons are on, a small **extraction-disc legend** near the bottom-left map tools teaches gold vs brown disc colours (Chapter 3).
3. **Empire buttons** sit as an always-visible **icon column on the left** of the map (labels on hover): Production (**Production screen**), Development (**Development screen**), Trade (**Trade screen**), Civilian Units, Military Units, Naval Units, Diplomacy (**Diplomacy screen**), Technology (**Technology screen**), Victory (**Victory screen**). Icons are defined in the toolbar-icons catalog. Development scans improvable tiles empire-wide and one-tap assigns Builders (Chapter 6); Trade opens the Market + Deal Book desk (Chapter 8); Victory opens mid-campaign standings and conditions (Chapter 15); market clearance still resolves in turn phases.
4. The top bar carries turn controls, region tabs, and the hamburger that opens **Game side menu** (Game Parameters read-only + Debug log — not a second empire toolbar).
5. Tap a province or sea zone to open **Province sea-zone overlay** for identity, ownership, and local actions.
6. Map control affordances (stacked-layers cycle, home-to-capital, map options, region minimap, treasury display) use the same icon set; treat them as map chrome, not separate routes. **Map display options** names the information layers (**Show resources**, **Show improvements**, **Show roads and rails**) and cartographic chrome (**Show province and sea borders**, ownership, names). The stacked-layers button is a shortcut through four presets; its tooltip names the current combination. Preferences persist across save and load.

New-game setup may show a non-interactive progress wait-gate before you land here; Chapter 2 covers that flow.

## Counsel

**Counsel.** Hark, my liege: count Old World provinces toward glory, and treat the New World as a forge of wealth and leverage — not as the scoreboard itself.

**Tip.** Learn the left-rail empire buttons before you hunt every map glyph. Most decrees begin there or on a selected province.

**Warning.** Minor Nations look small on the map and feel large in war. Parity means their regiments keep pace with the best Great Power tech.

## The other courts

Rival Great Powers plan with the same order surface you do. Early turns they typically secure capital connectivity, civilian work, and cautious diplomacy while growth-stage goals still favor consolidation over reckless invasion . Blessed AI profiles chosen at setup only reshape personality parameters — not secret victory rules.

## Consequences

- Misreading Old vs New World ownership delays victory and wastes armies on tribes that do not move the 31-province needle.
- Ignoring the turn/year mapping makes calendar halt at 1800 a surprise.
- Living only on the map without the empire rail leaves Production, Diplomacy, and Technology underused while rivals advance.
